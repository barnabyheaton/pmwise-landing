> the most oxymoronic expressions I have heard in my career, but people produce vast reams of virtual

> "The sick joke is SAFE has created a much larger bureaucratic burden than the preceding models Agile
> was trying to fix, yet so many people who don't know this context are so proud of how agile they are
> as they speak about velocities and t-shirt sizes and traipse off to their third steering committee."

> "It's Monty Python meets Yes Minister."

> "Pure agile is build the next slice as best you can MVP, test, refine, iterate until it works the way
> you want, document and promote, then repeat with the next logical piece until the capability is
> complete. Not having all the answers up front becomes a strength because you begin with what you know
> and generate momentum, rather than getting paralysed trying to build a minutely detailed design
> before starting anything and finding it falls at the first hurdle. But the second method is how the
> vast majority of enterprise projects run. This is one of the driving forces behind my building this

> "I just worry the moment you have a team greater than one, by definition you get bureaucracy in the
> guise of communication/change/risk management and the model breaks. I think PMWise's exemplar clients

## 2. Research — a session's, 6 Oct 2026 (check before saying on camera)

### What the Manifesto is
- **Who:** seventeen software practitioners — Extreme Programming, Scrum, DSDM, Crystal, Feature-Driven
  Development, Adaptive Software Development, Pragmatic Programming — among them Kent Beck, Martin
  Fowler, Ward Cunningham, Ken Schwaber, Jeff Sutherland, Robert C. Martin, Alistair Cockburn, Jim
  Highsmith, Ron Jeffries and Dave Thomas. They named themselves "The Agile Alliance".
- **Where and when:** a ski weekend at The Lodge at Snowbird, Utah, **11–13 February 2001**, looking for
  an alternative to *"documentation driven, heavyweight software development processes"*.
- **What:** one page. **Four values** — the crawl material:
  1. **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
  2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
  3. **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
  4. **Responding to change** over following a plan

  *"…while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more."* Behind them
  sit twelve principles — among them: build projects around motivated individuals and *trust them to
  get the job done*; welcome changing requirements, even late; simplicity is *"the art of maximizing
  the amount of work not done"*. ⚠️ Quote the principles from agilemanifesto.org itself before filming.

### What it became — in the signatories' own words
- **Ken Schwaber** (co-creator of Scrum), **August 2013**: *"unSAFe at any Speed"* — SAFe as a rehash of
  the heavyweight Rational Unified Process the Manifesto was written against. Dean Leffingwell (SAFe's
  creator) replied defending it.
- **Dave Thomas**, **2014**, *"Agile is Dead (Long Live Agility)"*: the word had become a marketing label
  for anyone with hours to bill or products to sell; agility is a way of behaving, not a product you
  install.
- **Ron Jeffries**, **2018**: developers should abandon capital-A "Agile" — "Faux Agile", "Dark Agile",
  "Dark Scrum" — the forms that made developers' lives worse.
- **Martin Fowler**, **August 2018, keynote at Agile Australia**, *"The State of Agile Software in 2018"*:
  much of what is done is *faux-agile*; the first challenge is fighting the **"Agile Industrial
  Complex"** and its habit of imposing process on teams.

### Where his argument meets the Manifesto
- His three-year budget — exact on who/what/when/where/how, vague on the why — breaks **values 3 and 4**
  outright: the contract and the plan are fixed first.
- *"Exactitude on the why"* is PMWise's own position (benefit-driven; the landing page's *"from why to
  why not"*).
